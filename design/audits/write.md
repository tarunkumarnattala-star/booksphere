# Audit - Write `/book/[id]/create-discussion`

Before: `design/shots/write-before-390.png`, `write-before-1440.png`.

## 1. The one thing
**Which of the eleven kinds you are writing** - and then the writing. The kind is not
metadata; it is the product's argument about what is worth writing down.

## 2. Where it belongs
The three groups belong here and only here (the book page stamps a type, it does not teach
the taxonomy). But they were **inside a native `<select>` showing "Insight"**, so the one
screen that is supposed to make the argument folded it behind a chevron. The ordering work -
lived outcomes first, summary last - was invisible to every writer.

## 3. The reading path
Card, eyebrow, title, lead, a cream prompt box with a lightbulb, then six labelled grey
pills. The prompt box was the loudest thing on the page and it restates what the type
dropdown already said.

## 4. The grid
`max-w-5xl` (1024px) with a 32px-radius card inside it: a 900px-wide text field for prose
that will be read at 664px.

## 5. The spacing scale
8, 16, 20, 24, 32 within a card whose padding (24/32) is doing the margin's job.

## 6. The type scale
`title-1` 48px, 16px lead, 14px/500 labels, 15px fields, 12px counter, 10px caption. Field
labels at 500 weight are heavier than the body text they sit above.

## 7. Generic tells present
- One big rounded card holding the whole form.
- Grey filled pill inputs at 20px radius.
- A cream "tip" box with a lightbulb icon.
- A full-width pill submit button.
- "0/20 minimum characters" - machine phrasing.

## 8. Verdict
Yes. **AI-score 23.**

## What changed
All eleven types are on the page under their three group labels, in the product's order,
with the chosen one underlined in ink. The prompt for the chosen type sits under a rule as a
statement of the question being answered. Fields are ruled boxes with mono labels, the
writing field is 70 characters wide, and the four "what actually happened" fields are
labelled as one block rather than dropped in a grey tray. The page closes with the one thing
a first-time writer needs to know: "Free. You sign in with Google at this step, not before
it."
